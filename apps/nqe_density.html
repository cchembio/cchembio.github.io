Geometric isotope effect
—

⟨x⟩H − ⟨x⟩D. The two isotopes sit in the same potential and still have different average positions — the heavier one hugs the classical minimum more closely.

Hydrogen bond
Readouts
One slider does everything: as the donor and acceptor move apart, the barrier between the two proton sites rises and the wells separate. A single coordinate therefore controls both the shape of the potential and how much of it each isotope samples.
Barrier and well separation are tied to R by an illustrative relation, Vb = 140 (R − 2.35 Å)² kJ/mol and 2a = R − 2.00 Å. Not fitted to a specific hydrogen bond. The real geometric isotope effect in hydrogen bonds also involves the heavy-atom donor–acceptor coordinate itself responding to isotopic substitution — a relaxation this 1D proton model cannot represent. What is shown here is the proton's own shift in an asymmetric well: a legitimate 1D analogue, but not the same observable.
